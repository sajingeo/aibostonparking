Concept: A crowdsourced Boston parking app that tells you, in real time, whether you can park at a specific spot right now, based on the current time, date, and weather.

Core flow

Tap a spot on the map (near you or around a searched destination).
Data exists: you see a photo of the parking sign and a clear verdict, green “good to go” or red “not parkable,” right away.
No data: the app prompts you to upload a photo of the sign. Claude reads the sign, extracts the rules, and adds them to the database for future users.
The verdict comes from the sign rules plus the current time, date, and weather (for example, snow emergencies or street cleaning).
Spot detail view

Sign photo and a yes/no verdict first, with details below.
How long you can park right now (e.g., 2 hours max).
Cost: free, metered, or paid.
Difficulty level: how hard it usually is to find a space there.
Safety, walkability, and how close it is to public transit.
User reviews, with an AI summary.
Trust and verification

Thumbs up or down so users can confirm or dispute a verdict.
A visible count of how many people agreed.
A “verified” badge when a business or building owner confirms the info.
A clear source for each piece of data (crowd, owner, etc.).
Search and filters

Search a destination and see parking nearby, not just around your current location.
Filters for how long you need, free versus paid, and difficulty.
Personalized to your vehicle’s type and size, and possibly your plate number. This could matter for resident-permit zones.
Open questions

What “odd plate number” was meant to cover. Boston doesn’t use odd/even plate rules the way Jakarta does, so this could become resident permits or street-cleaning sides instead.
Whether safety and walkability data comes from users or public sources like crime data and transit maps.
The MVP scope for the hackathon. A tight demo could be: map, tap a spot, sign photo, Claude parses it, and a time-aware green or red verdict with thumbs up/down. Reviews, verified badges, and vehicle profiles could be shown as UI mockups only.
